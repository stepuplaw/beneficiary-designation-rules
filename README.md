# Beneficiary Designation and Trust Account Rules at US Financial Institutions (2026)

Beneficiary designation and trust account rules at 247 US financial institutions: brokerages and IRA custodians, the 100 largest banks, 401(k), 403(b) and 457 recordkeepers, life and annuity insurers, the TSP, the Florida Retirement System and large state pensions, credit unions, HSA custodians, and every Florida-headquartered bank and the 40 largest Florida credit unions. 365 institution and account-type listings carry 3,133 rules (whether a trust can be named, how the trust must be named, per stirpes, banned wording, spousal consent, the default beneficiary, what happens when a beneficiary dies first, how to submit), and 172 listings carry 620 rules for opening an account in a trust's name. Every value is backed by a verbatim quote from the institution's own form or agreement, with the URL, form number and revision date. Read September 24 and 25, 2026.

Live pages: https://stepuplaw.com/data/beneficiary-designation-rules/ and https://stepuplaw.com/data/how-to-name-a-trust-as-beneficiary/ and https://stepuplaw.com/data/trust-account-requirements/

## Files

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `data/beneficiary_rules.csv`, `data/beneficiary_rules.json` | One row per institution and account group, each rule with value, detail, verbatim quote, source URL, form number and revision date |
| `data/trust_accounts.csv`, `data/trust_accounts.json` | Rules for opening or retitling an account in a trust's name |
| `data/schema.json` | Column definitions |

"Not stated" means the institution's public documents are silent on the point, which is different from a refusal. Institutions' own documents are linked, not redistributed. This is reference information, not legal advice.
